Search "Yondr" and most results describe a pouch: a lockable bag that keeps a phone out of a student's hands during the school day. While pouches are a part of the Yondr program to be sure, many schools underestimate what they actually need to have a true phone-free school. The pouch is the visible part, while the program around it, built from a decade of running phone-free rollouts in thousands of schools across the world, is what decides whether a policy survives and thrives past its first semester.

## The Pouch Is Only Part of the Program

A pouch that keeps a phone physically inaccessible is only one part of creating a successful phone free school. The harder challenge is getting a building full of students, teachers, administrators, and parents to adopt a new daily routine and sustain it throughout the school year.

That is where the difference between buying a product and implementing a program becomes important.

With a product only approach, much of the work remains with the school. School leaders still have to develop the policy, determine how it fits within their existing discipline framework, communicate the change to parents and staff, train teachers, manage daily logistics, address exceptions and violations, replace lost or damaged equipment, and troubleshoot the inevitable situations that emerge once the policy meets the real world.

Those responsibilities create costs that don't appear in the price of the pouch: administrative labor, teacher time, training, communication, logistics, enforcement, replacement costs, and, most importantly, the risk that inconsistent implementation undermines the behavior change the policy was intended to create.

Yondr was built around solving that broader implementation challenge. After more than a decade of phone free rollouts, schools aren't starting from a blank page or figuring out the process on their own. A Yondr partnership combines the physical system with the experience, tools, and ongoing support needed to put it into practice.

That includes:

- **Policy consultation and development** tailored to the school's needs and existing discipline framework
- **Implementation planning** built around the school's calendar, operations, and culture
- **Stakeholder communication resources** for parents, staff, students, and other key audiences
- **Staff onboarding and training** on both the Yondr system and the policy supporting it
- **Launch and rollout support** to establish consistent routines from the beginning
- **Ongoing partner success support** throughout the school year, not simply at launch
- **Pouches and unlocking accessories** appropriately configured and sized for the school

The distinction matters because the goal isn't simply to put phones inside pouches. **The goal is to create a phone free environment that works consistently enough to change the culture of what happens inside the school.**

The pouch makes that environment possible. The program is what makes it work.

## What the Results Look Like in Practice

Across the schools and districts Yondr has worked with, the pattern shows up consistently in both staff and student feedback and in the numbers schools report back:

- 86% report a positive impact on safety and wellness
- 84% report a positive change in student engagement
- 72% report a positive change in student behavior
- Teachers report gaining an extra 20 minutes of usable instructional time per day, roughly 3,500 minutes a year of uninterrupted teaching

## What Makes a Program Different From a Product

Software tools and unsupported pouches both share the same limitation: the effect they produce only lasts as long as something is actively enforcing it, whether that's a teacher watching a dashboard or a student choosing not to try to defeat a cheap lock. A program changes the underlying behavior and culture, which is why the effect holds up even on the days no one is watching closely.
