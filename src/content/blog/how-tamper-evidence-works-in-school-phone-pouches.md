When principals first ask about phone pouches, the question is almost always the same: can students break into them? Given enough time, a teenager can damage almost anything a school hands out, and that’s true of every pouch on the market. Perhaps the most practical question is whether staff can tell afterward that tampering has happened.

Yondr pouches are durable and can withstand years of use under normal conditions, but are also built to be tamper-evident. If one has been opened, whether by accident or on purpose, staff can tell. This guide explains how the pouch does that, who is supposed to be able to open one under what circumstances, and how schools practically fit ppuch checks into an ordinary day.

## Tamper-Evident Does Not Mean Tamper-Proof

The FDA drew this distinction in 1998, when it [changed the wording](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1998-11-04/pdf/98-29388.pdf) of its packaging rules for over-the-counter medicines from "tamper-resistant" to "tamper-evident," on the grounds that the newer term better describes what packaging can actually do. The [federal definition](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-G/section-211.132) it adopted is still a good one: a tamper-evident package has "one or more indicators or barriers to entry which, if breached or missing, can reasonably be expected to provide visible evidence" that tampering has occurred.

The same arrangement is behind evidence bags, ballot box seals, and the sealed packets that exam papers come in. All of them can be opened, but opening one leaves a mark, and somebody at the receiving end is responsible for noticing it.

## Who Can Open a Pouch

A phone pouch is a form of access control. It sets out who can open it and under what circumstances:

- Students can't open it during the school day.
- Authorized staff can, at an unlocking base, at the times and places the school's policy allows.
- Anyone who forces it leaves evidence behind.

Judged this way, a lock that resists for a long time and then gives way without a mark isn't much use to a school, since nobody learns anything from it. A pouch solution that shows an attempt has been made to hack it gives administrators of the policies something to actually follow up on.

Access control doesn't mean taking the phone. Students keep possession of their own phone inside the locked Yondr pouch. Phones don’t need to be confiscated or collected, and it's never stored away from the student.

## How the Yondr Pouch Shows Evidence

Yondr's pouch closes with a proprietary pushpin lock, patented in the US and built from metal and plastic components inside a durable outer. The current pouch uses a thicker pin and a stronger locking mechanism. Yondr doesn't describe it as unbreakable; the design goal is that deliberate damage is visible to staff.

The lock is also integrated into the pouch rather than attached as a separate assembly. That distinction is worth asking any vendor about, because a lock attached as its own part introduces a seam, and a seam is where a closure can come away from a pouch without the lock itself being defeated.

## How Schools Use the Evidence

Schools that run pouches well attach the checking to moments that already exist in the day, so nobody has a new inspection to run:

- **At arrival.** Students put their phone in the pouch and lock it at the door with staff watching. [Troy City School District](https://www.troycsd.org/yondr/) in New York publishes this routine for its middle and high schools.
- **During the day.** A visual check takes a few seconds (is the pouch there, closed, and intact?). [Park City School District](https://www.pcschools.us/about/yondr-pouches) in Utah writes visual checks into its pouch guidance.
- **When something looks wrong.** A damaged pouch is handled under the school's existing code of conduct, the same as any other conduct matter, and the pouch is replaced.

Settling the phone question once, at the door, means teachers aren't asked to police phones every period, and designing that routine with a school is part of a Yondr partnership. ## Medical Access Is Part of the Design

Some students need to reach their phone during the day, for a glucose monitor or another medical device that pairs with it, and for them Yondr offers a Velcro pouch the student can open directly. [Colorado Springs School District 11](https://www.d11.org/boe/cellpolicy) has written this into policy and provides a non-locking Velcro pouch to students with documented medical conditions.

## What to Ask Any Pouch Vendor

- If a pouch has been opened, will my staff be able to tell?
- Is the lock built into the pouch, or attached as a separate part?
- What do you offer students with medical needs?
- How much help do you give with arrival checks, and with what happens when a pouch turns up damaged?

## FAQs

### Can students open a Yondr pouch?

A locked pouch is opened at an unlocking base, and staff control those. Tamper-evident isn't the same promise as tamper-proof, though. If a pouch is forced, the damage is visible and the school can respond.

### What should staff do when a pouch looks tampered with?

Handle it under the school's code of conduct, as with any other conduct matter, and replace the pouch. Yondr's implementation team helps schools set those steps before launch, so staff aren't working it out on the day.
