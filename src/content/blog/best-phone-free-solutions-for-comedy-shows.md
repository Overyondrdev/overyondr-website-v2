Comedians spend months working a new hour in clubs before anyone films it, and a shaky clip posted from the third row can spend a punchline for everyone who hasn't seen the show yet. That's a big part of why so many comedians have gone phone-free, and why the method matters so much in a comedy room.

This ranking compares five options for comedians, tour managers, and club owners, looking at how well each protects material, how it suits rooms of different sizes, what the night is like for the audience, who staffs it, and whether it can follow a comedian from one city to the next.

## Does a Comedy Show Need Phones Locked Away?

Some rooms get by on a host's announcement and a door staff that pays attention. The shows that need more than that tend to have a few things in common:

- New material is being worked out, and it isn't ready to be judged from a clip.
- A special is being taped, or is about to be.
- The room is small and dark enough that a single lit screen is visible from the stage.
- The club books touring headliners who make a phone-free room a condition of playing.

Some clubs have simply made it permanent. [The Comedy Store](https://thecomedystore.com/information) in Los Angeles has run every show device-free since December 2024, with phones and smartwatches secured in Yondr cases and Phone Use Areas set aside inside the venue.

## Brand Overviews

### Yondr

Yondr facilitates [phone-free shows and tours for comedians](/spaces/live-events) across clubs, theaters, arenas, and amphitheaters all over the world. It is the standard for phone-free live entertainment, used by artists including Dave Chappelle and Bruno Mars to keep audiences present. Chris Rock, Kevin Hart, Marcello Hernández, and Matt Rife have all run shows with Yondr pouches, and across every kind of event the company’s pouches have been used at [more than 10,000 events](/blog/billboard-article-1025).

Unwanted recording is a problem for anyone trying out new jokes, tightening a set, or protecting a tour's worth of material ahead of a special, and when the audience's phones are locked in pouches they keep with them, a comedian can take a risk on stage without it following them home.

Guests hold on to their phone throughout. Anyone who needs it steps into a designated Phone Use Area, and at the end pouches open at unlocking bases by the exits. Yondr supports events at any scale, from stadium tours and comedy specials to corporate events and weddings, with on-site staffing or ship-to-venue options; the same system covers a week of club warm-ups and the arena run that comes after.

- **How it works:** Phones locked in pouches at entry; opened at unlocking bases at the exits
- **Audience access:** Guests keep their phone; designated Phone Use Areas inside the venue
- **Beyond the product:** On-site Yondr staff or ship-to-venue, with show-day planning and support
- **Who it suits:** Comedians, tours, and clubs that need new material kept in the room

### Lockabl

Lockabl is a UK company that rents and sells lockable phone pouches to events, venues, and schools, and it markets per-show rentals to comedy shows specifically. Guests seal their own phone into a padded pouch on the way in and keep it with them; a magnetic unlocker opens the pouches on the way out. Rentals are sized to the venue and come either as a self-service kit or with Lockabl staff on site. Delivery is within the UK.

- **How it works:** Guests seal phones in pouches at entry; opened with a magnetic unlocker at the exit
- **Audience access:** Guests keep their phone with them
- **Beyond the product:** Self-service hire or a managed service with on-site staff

### Cam-Block (ID Card Centre)

Cam-Block stickers come from ID Card Centre, a UK ID card supplier. Staff place a low-tack label over each camera lens as guests come in, and if one is peeled off, the word VOID is left on the phone. Guests can still use their phones for tickets, messages, and payments, so only the camera is covered. A sticker discourages filming and makes removal easy to spot, though nothing physically stops someone from peeling it.

- **How it works:** Tamper-evident sticker over camera lenses at the door
- **Audience access:** Full phone use except the camera
- **Beyond the product:** Supplied in packs; custom printing available
- **Who it suits:** Small clubs that want to discourage filming without handling phones

## Which Approach Fits Your Room?

A small club that only wants to discourage filming can start with a sticker at the door and a firm host, which is cheap and asks very little of the audience.

A comedian building an hour, or a club that books them, needs the material to stay in the room. That takes phones out of reach while the audience still holds its own devices and the door keeps moving, which Yondr has done in clubs and in arenas.

## FAQs

### Why do comedians ban phones at shows?

Mostly to protect material that isn't finished. Jokes get built live over many shows, and a recording that goes up early can spoil a bit before it ever reaches a special. Comedians also say that an audience without phones reacts differently.

### Who sets the phone policy, the comedian or the venue?

Either one can. A touring comedian can write a phone-free requirement into the show terms, and the venue carries it out on the night. Some clubs, The Comedy Store among them, have made it house policy for every show.

### What if a guest needs their phone during the show?

At a Yondr show the guest steps into a designated Phone Use Area, where the pouch can be opened, and locks it again before going back to their seat. Yondr's [guidance for guests](/faq) suggests leaving the phone on vibrate, since a call can be felt through the pouch.
