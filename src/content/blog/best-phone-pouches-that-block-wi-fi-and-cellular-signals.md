Every signal-blocking pouch makes the same basic promise: put a phone inside, and calls, texts, notifications, and data stop reaching it. But the people buying these pouches want very different things from them. A traveler or privacy-minded owner wants a sleeve they control, one they can seal when it matters and open the moment it doesn’t. A school, testing center, or courthouse wants the opposite: hundreds of phones silenced at once, and confidence that none of them come out until someone in charge decides they should.

This ranking is written mainly for institutions, with notes for individual buyers where their needs differ. We judged each of the five brands on how well it blocks signals and whether the maker has published testing to prove it, whether the pouch locks and who holds the key, what kind of support the company provides, and how it sells the product.

## Do You Need Signal Blocking, a Lock, or Both?

Locking a phone away and blocking its signal solve two different problems. A locking pouch keeps the phone out of someone’s hands; a signal-blocking pouch keeps the phone itself silent and offline. Many phone-free schools need only the first. Once a student can’t reach their phone, it has already stopped being a distraction, whether or not it’s still receiving texts.

Organizations that need signal blocking usually have a specific reason:

- An RFP, district policy, or exam rule requires it.
- Stored phones keep buzzing and chiming, and the noise is disrupting the room.
- Students are hotspotting a phone to a laptop, or screen mirroring, to reach sites the school network blocks.
- A court needs to keep jurors and witnesses from receiving outside messages or looking up information about the case during proceedings.
- The setting is an exam, a hearing, or a confidential meeting, where a connected device is a risk even when nobody is holding it.

Airplane mode won’t cover these cases. For one thing, it relies on every single person turning it on and leaving it on. For another, [NIST’s guidance on mobile device forensics](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/specialpublications/nist.sp.800-101r1.pdf) notes that airplane mode doesn’t always shut off services such as GPS. A shielded pouch works the same way no matter how the phone is set, as long as it’s fully closed, since any gap in the closure lets signal through.

## FAQs

### Do signal-blocking pouches block both Wi-Fi and cellular?

Yes. A Faraday pouch wraps the phone in conductive material that blocks radio signals going in and coming out, and that covers cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, GPS, and NFC. Pay as much attention to the seal as to the fabric, though, because shielding only works when the pouch is fully closed.

### Is a signal-blocking pouch the same as a signal jammer?

No. A jammer transmits radio energy that disrupts other people's devices, and the [FCC states](https://www.fcc.gov/general/jammer-enforcement) that federal law prohibits operating, marketing, or selling jamming equipment, with no exemption for classrooms. A pouch transmits nothing and only shields the phone inside it.

### Does Yondr sell a signal-blocking pouch?

Yes. Yondr offers a Faraday signal-blocking pouch with a secure lock and a universal unlocking station, and it also sells its standard pouch, which locks but doesn't block signal. Buyers choose the configuration when they order, and the same implementation program comes with either.
