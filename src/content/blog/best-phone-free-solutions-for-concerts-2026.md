An artist who wants a phone-free show has more ways to get one than five years ago. Locking pouches, camera-lens stickers, phone lockers, and a plain request from the stage all promise the same room: faces up, hands free, nothing online before the encore. What they deliver on the night varies a great deal.

This ranking compares five options a tour manager, promoter, or venue can book today, looking at whether each one prevents recording or only discourages it, what the night is like for fans, who staffs the doors, and whether the approach has been run at arena scale.

## Does Your Show Need Phones Locked, or Only Discouraged?

A show does not always need the strictest option. A sticker over the lens or an announcement before the first song may be enough when the goal is courtesy. Shows that call for phones physically secured tend to have one of these in play:

- Unreleased songs, or a production the artist wants seen in the room first.

- A performer who plays off the audience and finds that a wall of screens changes the show.

- A taping, album playback, or special where stray footage carries a real cost.

- Unreleased material played live, or a warm-up show where an artist tests a new set before a bigger debut, as Justin Bieber did at the Troubadour ahead of his Coachella headline set.

- A venue that has tried signs and announcements and watched them fail by the second song.

If the goal is certainty, the phone has to be out of reach, and the rest of this piece is about who can do that for a full house without spoiling the night.

### Cam-Block (ID Card Centre)

Cam-Block stickers, sold by the UK supplier ID Card Centre, are low-tack labels placed over a phone's camera lenses at the door. They are tamper-evident: peel one off and the word VOID is left showing. Fans keep full use of their phones for tickets, payments, and messages. A sticker discourages filming and makes removal obvious to staff, but nothing physically stops a fan from peeling it.

- **How it works:** Tamper-evident sticker applied over camera lenses at entry

## Which Approach Fits Your Show?

If the aim is to discourage filming at a club night, a tamper-evident sticker is the least effort but the most difficult to enforce at scale. If fans simply want somewhere to leave a phone, lockers do that.

A show that needs recording off the table for a full house, with fans still holding their own phones and the doors moving, is a different job. Yondr is the entry here that originated the concept and has done it at more than 10,000 events, with its own staff on site when a tour wants them.

## FAQs

### What is a phone-free concert?

A show where phones, smartwatches, and recording devices are not used in the performance area. With a pouch system, each fan's phone is locked in a pouch they keep with them, so the policy holds without anyone handing a device over.

### Do fans keep their phones at a phone-free show?

With Yondr, yes. Fans keep possession of their phone for the whole event. The pouch locks it, and it is opened again at any exit when the show ends.

### What if a fan needs their phone during the show?

Yondr shows have designated Phone Use Areas inside the venue where pouches can be opened. Yondr's [guidance for fans](/faq) suggests setting the phone to vibrate, since a call or text can be felt through the pouch.

### Does unlocking slow down the exit?

Unlocking bases and staff are placed at each exit, and opening a pouch takes a moment. Yondr reports that leaving a phone-free show is usually quicker than leaving a regular one, because people are walking out with their eyes up.
