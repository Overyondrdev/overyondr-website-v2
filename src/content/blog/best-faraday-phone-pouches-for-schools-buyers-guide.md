A Faraday pouch is built around the promise to fully block the phone's signal, not just make it harder to reach. That promise is also the easiest one for a vendor to overstate, since "our fabric meets a shielding standard" and "our finished pouch was tested to block signal" sound identical in marketing copy but mean very different things for a classroom. This guide covers how to tell the difference and how the leading school-focused options compare.

## How to Evaluate a Faraday Claim

Two different things get tested in this category, and only one of them tells a buyer what actually happens once a phone is inside the finished pouch a school will use every day.

- **Weak disclosure:** a vendor names a standard the shielding fabric meets and stops there. The material may perform well on a lab bench, but nothing confirms the finished product, with its seams, closure, and stitching, performs the same way.
- **Strong disclosure:** a vendor reports the fabric and the finished pouch separately, each tested against a named standard (IEEE 299-2006, MIL-STD 285, or NSA 94-106 are the common references) across a stated frequency range in decibels.

Only strong disclosure tells a school what will actually happen in daily use, since a seam or a closure gap can leak signal even when the fabric itself is proven.

## Questions to Ask Before You Buy

- Was the fabric tested, the finished pouch, or both?
- Which standard was it tested against, and across what frequency range?
- Does the pouch also lock, or does it rely on the student to keep it closed?
- What happens for a student with a medical device that needs quick access?
- Is the price a one-time purchase or a recurring per-student fee, and what does it include beyond the pouch itself?

## FAQs

**What does "Faraday" actually mean for a phone pouch?**

A Faraday pouch surrounds a phone in conductive material that blocks electromagnetic signals, including cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, GPS, and NFC, from reaching or leaving the device while it's sealed inside.

**Is a more expensive Faraday pouch always better shielded?**

Not necessarily. Price often reflects durability, closure design, and included support more than shielding strength. The only reliable signal is whether the vendor publishes finished-pouch testing against a named standard, not the price tag.

For more on how Yondr's own pricing model works, see [What Yondr Costs in 2026: Purchase Pricing vs the Old Subscription](/blog/what-yondr-costs-in-2026-purchase-pricing-vs-the-old-subscription).

**Do all Faraday pouches also lock?**

No. Shielding and locking are separate features. Some pouches block signal but rely on Velcro or a zip closure a student could reopen; others combine shielding with a tamper-evident lock. Check both separately when comparing options.
